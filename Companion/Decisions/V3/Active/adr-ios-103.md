## ADR-IOS-103: Four Graph Requests per Outlook Mailbox, and Retry-After Is Honoured

**Status:** Active (2026-11-08, tabmail-ios #426).

**Context:** Microsoft Graph's Outlook service limits apply per app ID and mailbox: "Four concurrent requests", and 10,000 requests in a 10-minute period (learn.microsoft.com/graph/throttling-limits, "Outlook service limits"). The limit is documented there, so no live measurement is needed: the gate stays at four whatever Graph would answer to a fifth request, and a `Retry-After` in a form the app does not read falls back to the exponential back-off. A throttled request gets 429 with a `Retry-After` header, and Graph asks the client to "Wait the number of seconds specified in the Retry-After header" (learn.microsoft.com/graph/throttling); 503 Service Unavailable is also documented as retryable after a delay (learn.microsoft.com/graph/errors). Gmail documents 429 (per-user limits, including mail sending, "with a retry time") and 500/503 `backendError` as retried with exponential back-off (developers.google.com/workspace/gmail/api/guides/handle-errors). The app sent Outlook body fetches 25 at a time during backfill, on top of sync, actions, sends and calendar calls for the same mailbox, so concurrency was the limit it reached first. `performHTTPRequestWithRetry` ignored `Retry-After` (fixed 1 s, 2 s, 4 s back-off) and the Graph policy retried 429 only.

**Decision:**
1. **One FIFO `HTTPConcurrencyGate` per mailbox** (`Shared/HTTP/HTTPConcurrencyGate.swift`), limit `GraphConfig.maxConcurrentRequestsPerMailbox` (4). `GraphMailboxGates.gate(for:)` keys it by the lowercased address. `ExchangeProvider` and `ExchangeCalendarProvider` each look it up from the mailbox address they are built for, so mail and calendar count against the same four and no construction site can forget to share it. `acquire` takes a free slot or queues in one decision under one lock; `release` hands its slot straight to the oldest waiter, so a slot is never free while anyone waits. A caller cancelled while queued keeps its place, is admitted in turn, fails at once and releases.
2. **A slot is held only while a request is on the wire.** `performHTTPRequest` wraps `session.data(for:)` in `gate.withSlot`; a retry's sleep and a token refresh hold none, so a throttled request never blocks the mailbox while it waits. Every leg goes through it: the retry loop, the token-refresh retries (`AuthedHTTP`, `deleteDraft`, the calendar's `request`), and the upload-session `PUT` of a large attachment.
3. **`Retry-After` is honoured, within a per-call budget and the caller's deadline.** `HTTPRequestResult.retryAfter` carries delta-seconds (an HTTP-date is ignored). A retry waits that long, and without the header the exponential back-off is unchanged. The waits of one `performHTTPRequestWithRetry` call add up to at most `HTTPConfig.retryWaitBudgetSeconds` (10): a retry whose wait would pass it is not made, and the refusal goes back to the caller, whose own retry (next sync pass, next queue drain) comes later. The header-less back-off (1 + 2 + 4 s) fits inside it. The budget bounds sleeping only, and a queued action also has a deadline: it runs under `withTimeout(SyncConfig.pendingOperationTimeoutSeconds)` (15 s), which cuts off whatever is still running at that moment as an uncounted timeout, so a refusal that is slow to arrive (a 503 after several seconds) would be retried into that cut. `AccountOperationExecutor.executeSingleOp` therefore sets the generic task-local `HTTPDeadline.current` to the same deadline around the operation, and `performHTTPRequestWithRetry` makes a retry only if its wait plus one more request, as long as the last one took (slot wait included), ends before it; otherwise it returns the refusal it holds. A refusal received in time reaches the queue as a refusal (`retryCount` charged, toward exit 5) rather than as a timeout, however slowly each refusal arrives. A request still on the wire at the deadline has an unknown outcome and stays a timeout: an action that makes several requests (`setUserLabel`'s category read then its `PATCH`; a move out of the Inbox, whose legacy-category strip comes first) whose requests are themselves slow can still pass the deadline; it then reaches the queue as an uncounted timeout and is retried with the intention kept, which heals once Graph answers in time. Sync passes and the notification service extension set no deadline and keep only the budget; a notification service extension read that makes several requests can pass the extension's watchdog, which then delivers its partial result.
4. **`HTTPRetryPolicy.graph` retries 429 and 503.** Other 5xx are not retried by the HTTP layer.
5. **Outlook body fan-out is `GraphConfig.bodyFetchConcurrency` (2)** per backfill or body-indexing stream, below the limit, so one stream never takes every slot; other requesters of the mailbox (the body queues' batches, sync, actions, sends, calendar) share the rest in arrival order. `ExchangeProvider` applies it inside `fetchBackfillBatch` and `fetchTextBodiesStream`: the width the sync engine asks for is a ceiling, and the sync engine knows nothing of Graph.
6. **A send refused with 429 or 503 is transient**, for Outlook and Gmail alike: it stays queued with no strike, as an offline send does, and goes out once the server accepts it. Both services document these refusals as "retry later", and neither says the message is wrong. The transport owns which refusals mean that (`HTTPConfig.retryLaterStatusCodes`, read through `HTTPError.asksToRetryLater`); `AccountManager.isTransientSendError` only asks. The HTTP layer may already have retried the refusal (Graph's policy covers both; Gmail's covers 429, and its 503 is left to the queue). Other 5xx stay strikes, because a server error can repeat for the same message every time.

**Provider boundary (ADR-IOS-060):** everything Graph-specific (the four-request limit, the gate lookup, the fan-out width, the retry policy) lives in the transport (`Shared/HTTP`) and the Outlook providers. Above `EmailProvider` the only changes are the generic "asks to retry later" question in the send classifier and the queue's `HTTPDeadline`, a deadline every HTTP provider honours alike; `AccountManager`'s Outlook calendar factory passes the account's address, nothing Graph-specific.

**Known and bounded:** the notification service extension is a separate process and cannot share the app's gate. It makes up to two short Graph requests per notification, so at worst a fifth request is briefly in flight for that mailbox; Graph answers it with 429 and `Retry-After`, which the shared retry code honours within its budget, and the next request goes through. Nothing is lost, and it heals on its own. `MicrosoftAuthService.fetchMicrosoftUserInfo` (one `GET /me` at sign-in and calendar setup) is outside the gate too, one request each time. A cancelled request gives its slot back when its caller returns, which can be before the transport has torn it down and before Graph has finished it; right after a cancellation Graph can therefore briefly count a fifth request and answer 429, which the retry honours within its budget. Gmail keeps its own policy (`[429, 403]`), now also honouring `Retry-After` within the same budget. The ordinary action queue still counts a final 429/5xx toward `SyncConfig.pendingOperationServerRefusalRetryLimit` (the bounded fifth exit of the never-drop rule); the HTTP-layer retries make reaching it rarer, and the queue's `HTTPDeadline` keeps every refusal received in time visible to it (an action whose requests are still on the wire at the deadline reaches it as a timeout instead; Decision 3).

**Rejected:** a global request-rate limiter (the binding limit is concurrency, and the 10,000-per-10-minutes budget is not approached by one device); retrying every 5xx (a deterministic 500 would be sent four times per attempt); holding the slot through the retry sleep (one throttled request would idle a quarter of the mailbox's capacity for its whole `Retry-After`); a cap on each single wait instead of on the call (three waits of 10 s each overran the queue's 15 s deadline, turning a counted refusal into an uncounted timeout); bounding the call by a request count or by its sleeping alone instead of by the caller's deadline (a refusal that takes seconds to arrive still ran past it); dropping the inline retries for queued actions (a short `Retry-After` would then wait for the queue's next drain instead of seconds); passing the gate into the providers from `AccountManager` (a construction site that forgot it compiled and got four private slots); reusing `LLMSemaphore`, which counts the same way (it lives in the app target only while `Shared/` is also built into the notification service extension, it carries AI-specific configuration and logging, and it has no scoped slot that is given back on every exit); scoping the transient send rule to Outlook by provider type (provider knowledge above the boundary, and Gmail documents the same refusals as retry-later).

**Tests:** `GraphThrottlingTests` — gate limit, FIFO order, release on throw, and a caller cancelled while queued leaking no slot; per-mailbox gate identity; `Retry-After` honoured and parsed, each refusal's own `Retry-After` waited for, a wait past the budget not made, and the waits of one call stopping at the budget (inclusive) so a single-request queued action's last refusal arrives under the queue's deadline as a refusal; only 429 and 503 ask to be retried later; 503 retried and 500 not under `.graph`; a calendar 429 retried after its `Retry-After`; no slot held during a retry sleep (a second request reaches the wire while the first waits out its `Retry-After`); mail and calendar providers built from one address (any case) never exceed four requests on the wire under backfill, actions, sends and calendar listings, the four written out as Graph documents it rather than read from `GraphConfig`; every request leg (the token-refresh retries of an ordinary request, of the 404-tolerant probe and of a calendar request, a draft delete, an upload chunk) held to four; Outlook backfill and body streams asked for width 25 run two wide; 429/503 sends transient and 500 not, for Outlook and Gmail; a seeded fuzz with random latency, 429/503 refusals and chaos points, each round run once as it is and once with random cancellation, checking that the first reaches exactly four on the wire and never more (a cancelled request is returned to its caller before the transport tears it down, so with cancellation the fake can briefly count one more), that every operation not cancelled ends in success or an injected refusal, and that no slot leaks (`GRAPH_THROTTLE_FUZZ_SEED` replays one seed, `GRAPH_THROTTLE_FUZZ_ROUNDS` widens the search). `GraphThrottlingQueueTests`, through `AccountManager`: a queued mark-read whose 503s each take 3 s to arrive, with and without `Retry-After`, is charged one refusal before the 15 s deadline with its intention kept, while a label action whose second request is still on the wire at the deadline times out uncharged with its intention kept; a persisted Outlook or Gmail send refused with 429 or 503 stays queued with its content, no strike and no `sentAt`, and goes out on the next drain once accepted, while a 500 takes a strike; the providers `connectAccount` builds for an Outlook account, ordinary and calendar-only, share the mailbox's four with its other requesters. Acceptance: with `withSlot` releasing only on success (a slot leaked by every cancelled request), the fuzz fails. A test that injects a transient Graph refusal to see what its caller does answers `FakeHTTP.CannedResponse.graphUnavailableBeyondRetry` (503 with a `Retry-After` above the budget), so the HTTP layer does not retry it away inside the one request.
