## ADR-IOS-101: Dictation Context Terms Are the Words a Shipped Word-Frequency List Sees Rarely

**Status:** Active (2026-11-05; the data source changed from wordfreq to FrequencyWords and the cutoff from 40 to 35 on 2026-11-06, owner decision for licensing, as Voice's ADR-DESK-038). Owner direction via issues #383 and #398: the term check must never block the main thread, and iOS must behave exactly as TabMail Voice does; after research the owner chose per-language lists of frequent words ("the word list is the one we should go") over a spell checker, kept simple ("it's okay not to be perfect, this is just word biasing"). Replaces this ADR's first decision (below, `UITextChecker`; merged in #397, never released). Amends ADR-IOS-086 decision 2 (the rule that picks the terms) and follows TabMail Voice (tabmail-voice #364, ADR-DESK-038).

**Context:** the earlier rule took a capital inside a word, or at its start where no sentence starts, minus a fixed English everyday-word list, so a name at a line start was missed and only English was known. The first replacement asked `UITextChecker`, which is main-actor isolated: its first use cost 0.3 to 0.85 s on the main thread (a freeze), it answers "correct" for every word of a language it cannot check, and it treats other scripts as known. A shipped list has none of these.

**Decision:**
1. **The rule (identical to Voice's `contextTerms.ts`).** Every word on screen is checked: split on whitespace (JavaScript's `\s` and the next-line character), trimmed of the punctuation around it, kept when it has a letter, no character of Unicode category N, is no part of an address (`@`, `://`, compared scalar by scalar) and is at most `dictionaryWordMaxChars` (50 UTF-16 units; a longer word is never a term, so it is not asked). A word is **ordinary** when the word list sees it at Zipf `screenTermCommonZipf` = 35 (tenths; once in about 300,000 words of subtitles) or more, and a **term** otherwise. A word in a script no list is written in is a term only with a capital letter (uppercase or titlecase). Each word stands alone: no runs of names. Terms are ordered rarest first (a word no list has, or a script without a list, counts as 0), then by how often they are on screen, then by first appearance; the same word in another case is one term, spelled as first seen; at most the vocabulary has room for beside the user's dictionary; each a valid dictionary word of at least `correctionMinWordLength` characters. A word the lookup gives no answer for is never a term.
2. **No preferred languages, no language detection.** One combined list of the 41 languages' lists (FrequencyWords' OpenSubtitles 2018 counts; each word keeps the highest Zipf any list gives it): "check against all the languages we have and send what is not there". A name that is a common word in any of them is not sent (accepted). Korean words are judged as written, particles and all (#399). There is no Japanese list (Voice #367). The sentence-start rule, the everyday-word list and `Rule.legacy` are gone from this picker (`correctionCommonWords` stays for the corrections).
3. **The data** is TabMail Voice's, copied unchanged into `TabMail/Vendor/wordlist/` (`words.txt`, `languages.json`, `README.md` with the sources and the CC BY-SA 4.0 attribution; Voice PR #369) and bundled as a folder resource. Voice's `generate.py` rebuilds it; iOS never shares code with Voice, only these data files and `word-list-cases.json` (the cases both apps run).
4. **The lookup** (`WordList`) is a Swift type over the memory-mapped `words.txt` (sorted by UTF-8 bytes) searched by bisection: no UIKit, no main actor, nothing loaded up front, so it holds the main thread for no time at all, including the first dictation after launch. A word is looked up in each form the lists written in its script store their words in (`languages.json`: NFC or NFKC, letter tables for Serbian's Cyrillic and Chinese's Traditional, abjad marks removed, Turkish's i, Romanian and Turkish s and t with a comma or cedilla, then full case folding, NFC, straight apostrophe) and gets the most any of them sees it; a word it does not have whole is split at the joiners (hyphen, apostrophes, Catalan's middle dot, the zero-width non-joiner and joiner) and is as often seen as its least seen part. ICU (`libicucore`, through the bridging header) supplies a character's script and full case folding, which Swift's standard library lacks. A word whose scripts no list is written in, or that mixes scripts no language mixes, gets `.noList`.
5. **The controller** picks the terms in the detached task it already had (`DictationController.pickTerms`), asking the injected `ScreenWordFrequencies` (default: `WordList.bundled`) once for all candidates. The `contextTermsWait` = 0.2 s stays: a late lookup sends no terms; a cancelled one (the dictation ended) sends none and stops; words it did not answer for are never terms. The lookup gives the thread back every `wordListYieldWords` words.
6. **No cap on the number of candidates** (owner 2026-11-05); the cost is the lookup's, microseconds a word.

**Consequences:**
- Names that open a line or sentence are terms; weekdays, months and big cities ("Monday", "Paris") are ordinary.
- A name that is also an everyday word in one of the languages ("Mark", "Smith", "Apple") is not sent: the speech model spells it anyway.
- The app grows by the list (about 38 MB installed, less compressed). The files are CC BY-SA 4.0; the attribution is on the Acknowledgments screen (FrequencyWords, OpenSubtitles 2018, wordfreq, OpenCC, Unicode CLDR) and in `TabMail/Vendor/wordlist/README.md`. Bundling all languages is blessed by the owner (2026-11-06: \"bundle yes ... we bundle large network weights already. this is tiny\").
- Korean particles make some ordinary words terms (#399). A script without case and without a list (Thai) gives no terms, since it has no capital.
- The picker no longer depends on the device's dictionaries or language settings, so it behaves the same on every phone and on the simulator.
- The picking logs (debug-gated) the candidate count, the answers and the time it took.

### Superseded 2026-11-05 — the first decision of ADR-IOS-101 (system spelling dictionaries, PR #397, never released)

**Status:** Active (2026-11-05). Owner request via issue #383 (a name that opens a line, "Xyvora Brevalle  4:12 PM", lost its first word, so the speech model never got it); owner answers 2026-11-05: guard the dictionaries, all preferred languages, keep the 200 ms budget with the main-actor hop inside it, simulator is enough. Amends ADR-IOS-086 decision 2 (the rule that picks the terms) and follows TabMail Voice's change of the same rule (tabmail-voice #333, ADR-DESK-038).

**Context:** the earlier rule took a capital inside a word, or at its start where no sentence starts, minus a fixed English everyday-word list. A name at a sentence or line start was missed, and English words were the only ones recognised.

**Decision:**
1. **The rule.** A word is a term when it has a capital (or is in a script without case), no digit, and its lowercase form is unknown to the spelling dictionaries of the user's preferred languages (`Locale.preferredLanguages`, snapshotted when the dictation starts). The sentence-start rule and the everyday-word list no longer apply. Tokens are split on whitespace and trimmed of punctuation, runs of terms joined, a run longer than a dictionary word counted word by word, addresses (`@`, `://`) excluded, frequency order and the backend's word limits: all as before. `DictationContextTerms.candidates(in:)` lists the lowercase words that could be terms; `DictationContextTerms.terms(in:excluding:max:rule:)` judges them under a `Rule`.
2. **The dictionaries are `UITextChecker`** (`SystemSpelling.unknownWords`), asked word by word for each preferred language; a word is unknown only if every working dictionary flags it. No dictionary is shipped. It is main-actor isolated, so the whole batch of candidates goes over in ONE hop from the detached picking task (`DictationController.pickTerms`), inside the existing `contextTermsWait` = 0.2 s: a slower answer sends no terms, as before.
3. **The guard.** `UITextChecker` calls every word of a language it cannot check correct (measured 2026-11-04 on the iOS 26.5 simulator: Korean, Japanese, Chinese, Thai, an unknown id, and many languages it lists in `availableLanguages`, such as Polish, Czech, Finnish, Greek). So each preferred language is probed once per dictation with a made-up word (`DictationConfig.spellingProbeWord`), and a language whose dictionary does not flag it is left out. `availableLanguages` is not consulted.
4. **No working dictionary** among the preferred languages: the earlier rule, exactly (`Rule.legacy`): a capital inside a word, or at its start where no sentence starts, not an everyday word.

**Consequences:**
- Names that open a line or sentence are terms now. Weekdays, months and cities (lowercase unknown to English: "monday", "paris") are terms too; harmless (a phrase list only biases the model), but they take slots from the 200.
- Names that are also dictionary words ("Mark", "Smith", "Apple", "Lee") are not terms; the earlier rule picked them mid-sentence. Same as TabMail Voice's Hunspell rule.
- A Latin dictionary treats words of other scripts as known, so a Cyrillic or Greek name is a term only when no preferred language with a working Latin dictionary is set and one with a working dictionary for that script is (a bilingual English and Russian setup loses Cyrillic names, because a word is unknown only when every working dictionary flags it). Scripts without case and no working dictionary (Korean, Chinese, Japanese) yield no terms, as before.
- The Latin-script names on a screen of a user whose preferred languages have no working dictionary (Korean only, say) are picked by the earlier rule.
- The result depends on the device's dictionaries, which differ from the simulator's; checked in the owner's smoke test on a phone.
- The picking logs (debug-gated) the candidate count, the rule used and the time it took, to measure the hop.
